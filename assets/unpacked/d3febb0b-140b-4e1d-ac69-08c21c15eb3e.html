Problems
Every post was rewritten by hand for four channels — same idea, four formats, four rounds of edits.
Approval lived in chat threads: no record of what was approved, by whom, or which version went out.
Claims and tone drifted, because nothing checked copy against the brand guide before publishing.
Solution 1
One approved idea fans out into Facebook, Instagram, LinkedIn and Zalo copy through channel-specific prompt skills.
Solution 2
Approval becomes a gate, not a chat — two Telegram checkpoints log who approved what, with hold and revision paths.
Solution 3
Every draft is validated against the brand voice guide and claim register before it can reach a channel.

Proof of run
The gate, in practice

Each idea arrives in Telegram as a compact card — content ID, pillar, the idea itself, the evidence it cites, a claim check and a duplication flag — followed by four buttons. Approving from a phone takes seconds, and the choice is written straight back into the content queue.

Evidence citation on every idea, so nothing is invented
Claim check and duplication flag shown before approval
Four outcomes: approve all, approve one, request edit, hold
Gate 1 approval — tested via Telegram bot
Where it stands
Built and tested end to end — not put into operation

The full loop was run and behaved as designed. Rollout wasn't approved: the company's social presence is still small, and a monthly running cost for automation couldn't be justified at this scale yet. The system stays documented and ready to switch on when volume grows.
